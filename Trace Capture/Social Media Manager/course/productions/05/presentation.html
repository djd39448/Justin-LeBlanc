LESSON 05

Documents with distinct jobs

Choose the right document for authority, design, history, and handoff.

Video · Transcript · Captions

Visual outline
  1. One file, one job: Open the document that answers the question you actually have.
  2. Three design documents: Three related documents answer three different design questions.
  3. Execution memory: Execution documents let another person resume and assess the work.
  4. Document authority: Authority comes from the instruction and its scope, not its confident wording.
  5. Avoid a paper maze: The smallest clear structure is better than a maze of unnecessary files.
  6. Trace a change: Trace one changed truth through the documents that depend on it.
  7. The smallest useful set: A small project can keep the full workflow in a compact set of documents.
  8. Which rule controls?: Apply supersession to the actual conflict, not to the entire history.
  9. State vs. history: Current state, historical change and proof answer different questions.
  10. A restartable handoff: Make the next safe action easier to find than the story of the session.
  11. Follow a requirement: Follow one requirement from approval to implementation and proof.
  12. Conflicting documents: Resolve contradictory claims with evidence, not confident wording.
  13. The handoff test: Test the document structure with someone who was not in the conversation.
